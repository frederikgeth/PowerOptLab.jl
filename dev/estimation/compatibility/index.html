Measurement and electrical compatibility

The compiled estimator has a useful separation between topology, exact equations, measurement functions and numerical solvers. It remains a research prototype: its tests establish agreement on specific cases, not complete equipment coverage or production readiness. Prefer this API for new work.

One model, three solvers

Compile once with compile_state_estimator, create SEParameters, and solve with solve_compiled_state_estimator (dense composite step), solve_sparse_state_estimator (Hachtel/sparse QR), or solve_ipopt_state_estimator (classic nonlinear programming). solve_state_estimation(structure, parameters, x0) defaults to the sparse solver. The explicit solver names are preferred when comparing algorithms. All three consume the same residuals, exact constraints, first derivatives, source phasors, customer identities and priors. They report the same objective = sum(abs2,r) and dof = residual_count - observable_dimension - nuisance_dimension. Internally they may minimize half that sum; the public statistic is always J.

Ipopt receives sparse derivative patterns compiled from dependencies and the Gauss–Newton curvature H' * H. This approximation omits residual and nonlinear constraint second derivatives; it is not the exact Lagrangian Hessian. Residual, Jacobian and constraint evaluations are cached separately at each iterate. Every available primal point is checked for physical feasibility, reduced stationarity, rank and negative curvature, regardless of the optimizer's termination label. Its original termination and primal labels remain separate in diagnostics. Finite local checks do not establish a global optimum.

For floating networks, some KCL equations can be identities of the others. The shared Ipopt backend selects an independent equation basis using the full affine network/device coefficients plus independent nonlinear branch-current incidences. Sparse QR verifies those coefficient identities near roundoff. A bounded, synchronized cache reuses the proof when only readings, uncertainties or priors change; physical source/device changes require a fresh proof. It does not drop a physical equation just because its Jacobian is zero at the starting point. Original equations remain in evaluation.constraints and every final physical feasibility and rank check. solve_diagnostics reports both equation counts and the physical row indices sent to the optimizer. Feasibility scaling also includes amplification from the independent rows back to all physical currents, including transformer turns ratios. Inconsistent constant terms and weak grounding information are retained.

Compilation also omits return-current identities proved directly from explicit n-winding coil connections. Summing all phase and neutral terminal currents is exactly zero for a complete isolated winding, but summing rounded Ybus rows can manufacture a tiny coefficient. The structural proof applies only when all coil endpoints have equal component weights and no other touched element or device breaks the identity. Unknown element groups fail closed. Any finite electrode, however weak, retains its physical return equation. This conservative reduction leaves other element families to the numerical coefficient checks above.

The original solve_state_estimation(net, measurements) remains a separate native-engine compatibility API. It accepts Measurement only, requires paired customer P/Q coverage or explicit zero injection, and does not accept the newer measurement types or exact-device objects. Its source neutral must be explicitly grounded: otherwise the native engine would introduce an implicit earth bond. The native current-variable model supports overlapping delta/wye paths and merged customers with distinct returns. Only conflicting returns for the same original customer are rejected. The native observability diagnostic uses ForwardDiff, with analytic current elimination for overlapping paths.

A second contract difference remains explicit: native KCL imposes zero injection at every non-source conductor without an injection variable, including neutrals. It warns about undeclared conductors. The compiled model leaves such injections free and reports :undeclared_injection in preflight. Declare zero injections explicitly when comparing these APIs; missing telemetry alone is not a physical zero-injection declaration. Use the compiled API for finite source-neutral electrodes or incomplete conductor coverage.

Injection states and customer identities

compile_state_estimator(...; formulation=:auto) retains voltage elimination for simple disjoint customer paths. It adds one complex current per original unknown customer for merged customers, overlapping returns or mixtures of known and unknown devices on shared conductors. The latter include a known PV/charger on one phase and an unknown load on another phase sharing a neutral. Distinct original terminals preserve customer identity even when ideal switches merge their electrical nodes.

Select formulation=:current to use explicit unknown currents throughout, or :voltage to require the optimized elimination contract. Voltage entries remain [real(V_free); imag(V_free)]; current entries follow them. Use injection_state_indices(s) rather than guessing their ordering. Branch current is positive into the network; KCL is YV + I_exact - A*I_customer = 0. An explicit injection_connections path denotes an additional unknown customer, even when a known device has the same endpoints; its P/Q meters describe that unknown customer. Without an explicit unknown declaration, matching telemetry meters the exact device. Individual power is U*conj(I_customer), so its evaluator remains defined at zero connection voltage with finite currents. In contrast, the optional merged power profile requires nonzero voltage and retains its explicit domain check.

With explicit currents, missing customer channels may leave individual currents unobserved while total voltage remains identifiable. Compare identified quantities and selective covariance, not arbitrary unobserved current coordinates.

Measurement functions

TypeQuantitiesMeaning
Measurement:vr, :vi, :vmag, :pinj, :qinjVoltage or individual customer power; customer powers also declare their injection paths
NetInjectionMeasurement:ire, :iim, :imag, :pinj, :qinjTotal conductor injection into the passive network; no customer allocation and no inferred path
BranchMeasurement:ire, :iim, :imag, :pflow, :qflowCurrent or power entering one end/conductor of a finite line
TransformerMeasurementsame element quantitiesCurrent or power entering a transformer port/conductor
ShuntMeasurementsame element quantitiesCurrent or power entering a finite shunt, including an electrode
FunctionalMeasurementscalar user functionA function of selected rectangular conductor voltages, with automatic or supplied first derivatives

All readings and standard deviations are SI. Currents into passive elements are positive; customer injection power is positive into the network. Exact-device positive power denotes consumption. Element power uses conductor-to-earth voltage by default; reference="n" selects that bus's neutral instead. NetInjectionMeasurement current has no voltage reference. Unlike customer power, a net meter observes the sum at a merged electrical node. Individual customer power at a source-fixed conductor (including aliases) is rejected: the source reaction prevents separating that customer from the net current. Use NetInjectionMeasurement for net source telemetry instead. Declare injection_connections separately to retain unknown loads when telemetry drops out. A custom function also declares no injection path.

A functional callback receives [real(V[nodes]); imag(V[nodes])] in the supplied node order, including repeated nodes and zeros for ideal earth. Return one finite real scalar. ForwardDiff supplies its gradient unless gradient is provided; that callback must return one entry per input component. Functions must be pure, with immutable captured constants. Changing the function, node order or voltage reference requires recompilation; updating a reading or its uncertainty does not. Callbacks cannot introduce hidden current states, equipment equations or a non-Gaussian error model.

neutral_current = NetInjectionMeasurement(kind=:ire, bus="load", terminal="n",
    value=2.0, sigma=0.1)
electrode_current = ShuntMeasurement(kind=:ire, shunt="earth", terminal="n",
    value=0.4, sigma=0.05)
secondary_power = TransformerMeasurement(kind=:pflow, transformer="tx",
    port=2, terminal="1", reference="n",
    value=2000.0, sigma=30.0)
squared_voltage = FunctionalMeasurement(name=:squared_voltage,
    nodes=[("load", "1"), ("load", "n")],
    prediction=z -> (z[1]-z[2])^2 + (z[3]-z[4])^2,
    value=230.0^2, sigma=100.0)

The identifiers in this example must exist in the supplied network. These types are exercised by test/state_estimation_compatibility_tests.jl, including finite-difference derivative checks and agreement across all three solvers.

Transformer coverage

Finite-leakage single_phase, center_tap, wye_delta, delta_wye, single_phase_autotransformer, open_delta_regulator, and n_winding are supported within the electrical preflight contract. Regressions compare all three shared solvers and compare compatible node telemetry with native WLS. Transformer IDs are looked up across subtypes; variant is optional and only needed to disambiguate a duplicated ID. Ordinary transformer ports are 1/from and 2/to; center-tap port 2 includes both legs and the center conductor. An n-winding port is its winding-list index.

The open-delta shared phase has an ideal copper bond in addition to the finite winding primitive. Compilation identifies its two nodes exactly, matching the native engine. The bond is assembled upstream in BMOPFTools; PowerOptLab no longer inserts synthetic switches or maintains a separate admittance patch. Shared-phase port current includes an ideal-wire reaction and cannot be obtained from the winding primitive alone, so its element telemetry is rejected. Meter adjacent finite branches instead. N-winding telemetry is also rejected where different ports share the same original conductor and the exporter has already summed those currents. Other port currents use the original primitive order, including the distinct autotransformer ordering.

This is not support for every transformer model. Ideal/zero-leakage transformers, unrepresented vector groups/phase shifts, and automatic tap control remain outside the finite-primitive contract. Fixed supported taps are network parameters; recompile after changing them. Preflight rejects ignored or unsupported fields rather than treating them as implemented physics.

Neutral grounding and telemetry

Floating downstream neutrals, solid earth bonds, complex finite grounding admittances and multiple electrodes are regression-tested. Model finite electrodes as BMOPF shunts; their current and absorbed power are distinct from the current in a neutral line. A source-free case is tested with absolute voltage telemetry and with a remaining common-mode gauge. Grounding and a phasor reference are separate pieces of information.

An ideal grounded conductor has zero earth-referenced voltage and power, but may carry nonzero element current. Its net earth-reaction current is eliminated from reduced Ybus and cannot be measured using NetInjectionMeasurement. Element current telemetry remains available where a finite primitive determines it. Constant zero magnitudes have zero derivatives; a variable magnitude at zero is nonsmooth. Supply explicit magnitude_epsilon or current_epsilon when smooth approximations are appropriate, and account for that change in the model.

Compiled source phasors fix only the listed conductor-to-earth voltages. A floating phase-to-neutral voltage source with unknown common-mode voltage is a different model and is not implemented. Soil potentials, frequency-dependent electrodes and separate ideal-bond reaction-current states are also absent.

What the consistency tests establish

test/state_estimation_consistency_tests.jl adds references that do not simply generate an answer from the estimator's own Ybus:

The existing OpenDSS component/feeder matrix covers the other finite transformer families. These tests do not establish all configurations of each family or global uniqueness of a nonlinear fit. A common bug can survive agreement among solvers sharing one evaluator; independent circuit equations and external simulations are essential.

Use the same initial state, residual weights, injection paths, priors and stopping tolerances when comparing algorithms. The nonlinear comparison uses optimality_tolerance=1e-6 explicitly: dense/sparse currently default to 1e-8 and shared Ipopt to 1e-6. An overstrict request can correctly return a nonpublishable stall near floating-point resolution even when voltages and objectives already agree. Do not loosen a test merely to hide a physical constraint error.

For underobserved problems, compare predicted measurements and identifiable derived quantities, not arbitrary gauge choices. The tests verify that an independent stochastic prior resolves the gauge while preserving the uncertainty of measured voltage differences. An absolute voltage covariance that depends on an unresolved gauge must be rejected.

Projection roundoff is assessed against the original measurement Jacobian as well as the reduced map. This matters when exact device laws fix every differential voltage and only unobserved common-mode directions remain: H*Z is then zero mathematically, but its numerical entries need not be zero. The same rank convention governs shared solver statuses, diagnostics, unobservable directions and covariance. A quantity fixed by exact equations has zero local covariance. A weak real reference or prior remains information when it exceeds the projection's numerical resolution.

The mixed-winding statistical comparisons use explicit absolute-voltage readings when comparing native SI/per-unit WLS with the shared solvers. Those readings supply stochastic gauge information; no implicit neutral bond is added. Separate floating-circuit tests omit that information and compare only identified quantities. External component checks specify excitation explicitly, disable OpenDSS anti-float shunts and evaluate terminal equations at specified voltages, so an external solver's arbitrary gauge choice does not become ground truth.

ConstantCurrentDevice defaults to a fixed earth-frame phasor. Set angle_reference=:voltage for fixed magnitude and power factor relative to the connection voltage. Its angle is undefined at zero voltage unless the coefficient is zero. ZIPDevice retains a fixed-phasor current term; compose exact models when a voltage-following current term is required.

Remaining quality work includes nonlinear core/saturation models, further transformer configurations, broader uncertainty-coverage studies, realistic sparse telemetry and dropout sequences, and multi-start/conditioning benchmarks. A missing physical state or equipment equation needs a model extension; additional unit tests cannot manufacture that support.

Priorities for further work

  1. An augmented voltage/current formulation for ideal transformers, bond/source reaction telemetry, and floating phase-to-neutral source laws.
  2. Correlated measurement covariance, robust losses and statistically calibrated bad-data detection/identification. Raw standardized residuals alone do not identify bad data.
  3. Timestamp alignment, missing-channel management and explicit temporal process models. Sequential warm starts and priors are not a Kalman filter.
  4. Joint parameter/topology hypotheses, meter bias calibration and reproducible multistart studies. Local rank and convergence do not prove global uniqueness.
  5. Sparse rank/covariance factorizations, symbolic reuse and large-feeder benchmarks, followed by an import/export contract for meter identity, units, direction, references and covariance provenance.

Always inspect structure.preflight, solve_status(result).publishable and observability_diagnostics. A converged but underobserved result is feasible; it does not identify every voltage direction. A regularized continuation stage is not a publishable solution of the physical device law.

Statistical interpretation and numerical limits

J includes measurement and independent prior residuals; diagnostics also report the two components separately. In the optional voltage profile, fitted customer powers are nuisance parameters and are subtracted from dof. In the current formulation their estimated directions already enter rank(HZ), so they must not be subtracted twice. A chi-square interpretation is local/asymptotic and requires the stated independent Gaussian errors, correct physical model and properly counted independent prior information. Reusing correlated past data as an independent prior invalidates that interpretation.

The 500-draw regression covers one low-noise nonlinear circuit, not general calibration or bad-data processing. Final rank/covariance diagnostics still use dense SVD. Sparse derivatives and cached equation reduction do not establish large-network scalability. Native WLS current-observability diagnostics additionally compile twice and use dense Jacobians/null-space SVDs and pinv(A) to eliminate current directions. The demonstrated feeder range is 5–40 downstream buses.
