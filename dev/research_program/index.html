Research program

The research objective is to make distribution-network operating decisions with uncertain measurements and network models. This requires methods for identifying plausible models, selecting informative measurements and checking the resulting operating limits. The current package provides several of these methods separately; their combination remains a research objective.

The proposed sequence is:

telemetry and metadata
        ↓
model forensics → informative, safe interventions
        ↓                    ↓
plausible model ensemble ← new evidence
        ↓
verified operating decisions

Several network models may fit the same measurements. The useful questions are which parameters remain unidentified, whether the alternative models change an operating decision, and which additional measurements would distinguish them.

Current methods and limitations

The following methods are implemented. Their assumptions determine how the results can be used in a combined estimation and operating study.

StudyAvailable methodsMain limitations
Constrained state estimationNeutral-explicit state, exact equations versus stochastic residuals, tangent-space observability, selected local covariance, constraint multipliersNetwork identity and topology are fixed at compile time; there is no topology-hypothesis search
Operational parameter estimationShared line lengths and transformer taps over multiple snapshotsThe line prototype is single-terminal per end; it does not jointly infer full mutually coupled impedances, phase maps, switch states, or grounding models
Inverse Carson reconstructionEnumeration of overhead-construction candidates, compatible-candidate retention, local rank and profile intervals, materialized primitive four-wire modelsIt uses sequence linecode data, not joint operational telemetry, and does not combine candidates with the state estimator
Operating envelopesShared allocation across explicit forecast/model scenarios; bound-point, corner, and custom-utilization checksScenario models must be supplied by the caller; corner checks are not a global robust-feasibility certificate
Differentiated controller experimentLocal DiffOpt sensitivity of smooth lower-level PV/controller responsesDerivatives are conditional on the returned local solution and stable active set; discrete modes and alternative solution branches are not covered
Post-OPF operabilityNative static snapshot checks, scaling provenance, directional sensitivities, natural/pseudo-arclength continuation, local fold refinement and sufficient fixed-point certificatesGenerator/IBR/controller residual extensions and general global branch discovery are outside this scope
HELM power flowNo-load-connected branch with physical residual and series diagnosticsIt does not discover every power-flow branch or certify non-existence when its finite series fails

A compatible model fits the specified data; local observability concerns a particular operating point; corner feasibility concerns the tested combinations. The individual guides define these conditions and the numerical checks used.

Priority 1: joint network-model identification

Joint identification would combine the existing estimation methods to compare alternative network models. The uncertain model may include switch and neutral continuity, phase labels, transformer terminal maps and taps, line construction or mutually coupled impedance, source impedance, grounding, and meter sign, scale, or reference errors.

The comparison should report:

  • ranked discrete hypotheses and compatible continuous parameter regions;
  • locally unidentified directions and observational equivalence classes;
  • groups that are different physically but equivalent for a declared decision;
  • model discrepancies that affect the intended operating decision; and
  • candidate evidence that could distinguish material alternatives.

This requires comparing discrete configurations as well as fitting continuous parameters over the same operating points. The four-wire equations and measurement assumptions must be consistent across the comparisons. A local optimum alone does not establish unique identification.

Priority 2: differentiable experiment design

For a set of plausible network models, the next question is which measurements or operating-point changes best distinguish them: meter placement or synchronization, inverter active/reactive probing, a tap or capacitor action, or a suitable operating interval.

Candidate objectives include increasing the smallest identifiable singular value, reducing a selected parameter or decision covariance, and separating discrete hypotheses. Every intervention must also satisfy the same voltage, current, converter, and disturbance limits used for operation.

The existing methods supply residual Jacobians, local observability measures and sensitivities of selected operating responses. A complete experiment-design solver would also need to enforce equipment limits and check the validity of those sensitivities near changes in operating mode or solution branch.

Priority 3: counterexample-guided decision verification

The main limitation of the current operating-envelope calculations is that feasibility at the simultaneous bound, or even at every box corner, does not prove feasibility throughout a nonconvex AC utilization box.

The current assumptions and numerical checks are documented in the DOE problem reference. The detailed implementation audit and benchmark rationale remain in the DOE quantification scientific audit. The accompanying DOE literature evidence register separates established results, finite case evidence, reanalysis, and proposed experiments.

The next step is an outer allocation and inner counterexample search over both actual customer utilization and the plausible model ensemble. Each discovered violation becomes a new scenario for the allocation problem. Results should distinguish at least:

  • a falsification search that found a violating point;
  • a search-stable result for which no violation was found under a declared multistart and stopping policy; and
  • a certificate backed by a valid global relaxation bound.

A robust-feasibility certificate requires a valid bound covering the full utilisation and uncertainty set. A search that finds no violation establishes only the result of that search.

For a fixed polyhedral feasible region, non-enumerative box-containment machinery already exists in the literature. The open target here is narrower: use the nonlinear four-wire AC equations as the reference and test sensitivity-based screening without treating random utilization samples as a certificate, and eventually compare it with a valid linear robust inner-set baseline. A four-wire assessment of sensitivity filtering remains further research.

Foundational track: solution validity and differentiability

The inference and intervention program depends on local nonlinear solves. The static operability checker already provides local Jacobian diagnostics, finite-difference sensitivity checks, natural-parameter and pseudo-arclength continuation, local fold refinement, and sufficient fixed-point certificates within its native static scope. General multiple-solution discovery, controller/active-set transition handling, and KKT or continuation validation of DiffOpt sensitivities remain research extensions.

A reported sensitivity should identify the solution branch and operating mode, its local conditioning, and any nearby change in active constraints. Alternative local solutions may produce different sensitivities and operating decisions.

The Post-OPF voltage operability reference and study tutorial document the implemented processor. They separate endpoint feasibility, local Jacobian evidence, phase/sequence sensitivity, energized-base-connected reachability, and path-specific margin rather than compressing them into one stability index.

Further research topics

Possible extensions include:

  • Multiconductor relaxations and global bounds: develop SDP, SOCP, or QC relaxations for network identification and operating-envelope bounds; compare them against the neutral-explicit IVR model.
  • Hybrid controller equilibria: represent discrete taps, switched capacitors, deadbands, hysteresis, and interacting inverter controls when the question is reachability, multiplicity, or cycling—not merely optimal dispatch.
  • Fundamental-plus-harmonic optimization: add frequency-indexed primitives when harmonic voltage, neutral heating, resonance, or emission uncertainty changes a hosting-capacity or operating-envelope decision.

Moving-horizon estimation, decision-focused forecasting, safe learned local controllers, decomposition, and protection-aware envelopes are also plausible extensions when they close a demonstrated gap in this loop.

Research scope

The emphasis is on distribution-network estimation, controlled experiments and operating decisions with uncertain models. Additional device models, learning methods or solution algorithms should address a specific question in that programme. Their treatment of topology, grounding, uncertainty and model error should be stated alongside the results.
