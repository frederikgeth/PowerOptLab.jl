Choosing inverter and controller parameters

Every number passed to AdvancedInverter or a SequenceController is a modelling claim about hardware or firmware. This page explains how to choose each one so that a reader can audit it. Each parameter has one of three kinds of justification:

  1. derived from the model's own equations and a stated requirement, as below (preferred);
  2. sourced from a cited design reference or standard; or
  3. assumed, which then needs a sensitivity sweep in the study.

Bibliography entries marked † in IBR references have not been checked against live sources, and standard clauses have not been read from the licensed text. Treat every value attributed to them here as to be verified before publication.

Work in per unit of each device's rating

For a three-phase device with rating $S$ and nominal phase-to-neutral voltage $V_n$,

\[I_r=\frac{S}{3V_n},\qquad Z_b=\frac{V_n}{I_r}=\frac{3V_n^2}{S},\qquad Y_b=\frac1{Z_b},\qquad C_b=\frac1{\omega Z_b}.\]

A 6 kVA, 230 V inverter has $I_r=8.70$ A, $Z_b=26.5$ Ω and $C_b=120$ µF. Specify filters, gains and current headroom as fractions of these bases and convert per device. A fixed ohmic filter or a fixed A/V gain applied across a fleet of mixed ratings silently gives small and large inverters different physics and different control effort.

Output filter

The filter sets the internal-voltage drop, the converter-side carrier ripple and the LCL resonance. Liserre, Blaabjerg and Hansen give the usual design constraints (reference 14):

  • total series inductance at most about 0.1 pu, which bounds the fundamental voltage drop and hence the DC voltage needed;
  • midpoint capacitance at most about 5% of $C_b$, which bounds the capacitor's reactive power at fundamental frequency;
  • resonance between roughly $10f$ and $f_{sw}/2$; and
  • converter-side inductance large enough to keep the carrier ripple current within the semiconductor and inductor ratings.

Check the last constraint with the model itself: enable pwm_ac_ripple=true and read i_ac_switching_rms against the leg rating rather than relying on a closed-form ripple estimate. filter_resonance_hz reports the undamped LCL estimate.

For a reduced single-arm study, $X_f=0.05$–$0.10$ pu with $X/R\approx10$–$30$ is a defensible assumption: inductor resistance at line frequency is small, but not zero. For the 6 kVA example, 5% is $X_f=1.32$ Ω (4.2 mH). A value an order of magnitude smaller makes the internal voltage essentially equal to the POC voltage and removes the filter from the study; state that if it is intended. AdvancedInverter warns when a declared filter is below $10^{-3}$ pu.

DC-link voltage

From the switching hull in Scientific foundations, a balanced internal RMS voltage $|U|$ needs

\[V_{dc}\ge\frac{\sqrt6\,|U|}{m}\quad(\text{three/four-leg}),\qquad V_{dc}\ge\frac{2\sqrt2\,|U|}{m}\quad(\text{split link}).\]

Size $|U|$ at the highest supply voltage the inverter must operate at plus the filter drop at rated current. At 253 V (230 V +10%), a 5% filter, full reactive absorption and $m=0.96$, $|U|\approx266$ V. That requires $V_{dc}\ge679$ V for a three-leg bridge and $784$ V for a split link (derived). Unbalance increases the largest line-to-line or phase voltage; switching_margin reports the remaining headroom. Use the bus voltage of the actual product when it is known, and treat the model's limit as a check rather than a design.

DC-link capacitance and ripple-current rating

Capacitance and ripple-current rating are separate constraints. For a three-wire converter the 2ω power is

\[\widetilde S=3(U_1I_2+U_2I_1),\qquad C\ge\frac{|\widetilde S|}{2\omega V_{dc}\,\Delta V_{2}},\qquad I_{2\omega,rms}=\frac{|\widetilde S|}{\sqrt2V_{dc}},\]

for an open line-frequency DC source (derived; the source-branch generalisation is in the foundations page). The controller choice dominates this requirement. Take the 6 kVA example at 1% voltage unbalance with $V_{dc}=750$ V and a 1% (7.5 V peak) ripple allowance:

operating point$\lvert\widetilde S\rvert$required $C$$I_{2\omega,rms}$

plus $I_2=3$ A of negative-sequence supportup to ≈ 2.1 kVAup to ≈ 600 µFup to 2.0 A

A three-phase product sized for balanced operation therefore cannot be assumed to tolerate a negative-sequence law. Studies should either (a) use a sourced capacitance and ripple rating for a named product class and declare dv2_max and i_cap_max, or (b) treat capacitance as the hardware counterfactual and sweep it (see Fleet and hardware studies). A capacitance chosen so large that dv2 never binds removes the trade-off this model is built to expose; if that is deliberate, say so. Switching ripple, hold-up energy and control requirements add further lower bounds that the 2ω formula does not cover.

Current rating and headroom

i_max is the per-leg semiconductor (or inductor) rating, distinct from $I_r=S/(3V_n)$. To deliver rated apparent power at the lowest operating voltage $V_{min}$, it must satisfy $i_{max}\ge S/(3V_{min})$. At 207 V this is $1.11I_r$ (derived). A headroom of 10–20% is a common assumption; anything above the supply-voltage requirement is current available for negative-sequence support. With SequencePriorityLimiter(priority=:power) a phase can always carry at least $I_{max}-|I_1|$ of negative-sequence current.

Losses

Fit p_loss_fixed, a_loss and c_loss to a datasheet efficiency curve at several load points (for example 10, 20, 30, 50, 75 and 100% of rating) by least squares on $P_{loss}=P_0+a\sum|I|+c\sum|I|^2$. Report the fit residual. Losses now enter the DC-availability limit, so a lossless plant overstates delivered energy by the full converter loss. Capacitor ESR (esr_dc*) and its frequency weights come from the capacitor datasheet.

Volt-var and Volt-watt curves

Use the curve and the basis of the jurisdiction being studied. The AS/NZS 4777.2:2020 Australia A settings are commonly reported as follows; the remaining regional settings differ (sourced, to be verified against the licensed text, reference 47):

curveknots (V)ordinatesbasis
Volt-var207, 220, 240, 258+44%, 0, 0, −60%rated VA (q_scale = S)
Volt-watt253, 260100%, 20%rated power (volt_watt_basis=:rated)

A rated basis caps the curve ordinate at availability. A Volt-watt curve applied to available power, or with an earlier or lower knee, curtails more than the standard at partial irradiance. Such curves are legitimate sensitivity cases but should not be labelled as the standard.

For quantitative softplus corner deviations and width refinement, see the Australia A corner example. In particular, smoothing can put the requested volt-watt power above the original piecewise-linear ceiling near its lower corner.

Negative-sequence gain and angle

The static attenuation result in Phase-aware local control laws gives the design rule:

\[\frac{|U_2|}{|E_2|}=\frac1{|1+g\,e^{j\delta}|},\qquad g=\kappa_{tot}|Z_2|,\qquad \delta=\arg Z_2-\phi_2.\]

  1. Measure $Z_2$. Use the feeder's negative-sequence Thévenin impedance at the connection point, for example from inverter_control_network_voltage_sensitivity in sequence coordinates. Do not assume a typical LV angle: a short feeder behind a transformer is much more inductive than the cable alone.
  2. Choose the angle. $\phi_2=\arg Z_2$ aligns the response ($\delta=0$); any $|\delta|>90°$ can amplify unbalance. Report $\delta$ for every device.
  3. Choose the attenuation. For a target ratio $a<1$, $g=1/a-1$ when aligned; for example $a=0.8$ needs $g=0.25$. With a common per-inverter gain $\kappa$, bus $i$ sees $g_i=\kappa\left|\sum_jZ_{2,ij}\right|$, where the sum over the fleet's measured mutual impedances already accounts for the number of inverters. Do not divide by $N$ again: for $N$ inverters behind one shared impedance this is $g=N\kappa|Z_2|$.
  4. Check the current. $|I_2|=\kappa|U_2|$ must fit the headroom above. Express $\kappa$ in per unit of $Y_b$ so that heterogeneous fleets receive comparable effort.

A gain chosen without these steps can be a no-op ($g\ll0.1$), saturate every inverter, or point in the wrong direction. Each of these is a different study conclusion.

Sensing reference

Declare what the product measures: sensing_reference=:device (the plant's neutral, or ground for a three-wire plant), :ground, or a terminal name such as "n" for a three-wire power stage with a neutral sensing lead. Worst-phase laws respond to the zero-sequence voltage to first order and mean-phase laws to second order, so this choice changes results on feeders with neutral displacement.

Smoothing widths

Smoothing widths are approximation budgets, not tuning knobs. The defaults (extrema 0.05 V, curve corners 0.05 V, current widths $2.5\times10^{-5}$ of the rating, power widths $5\times10^{-8}$ of the rating) are starting values, not a universal current-error guarantee. Curve-corner error also depends on slope, power base and voltage; the Australia A corner example quantifies it. Use identical widths for every variant in a matched comparison, record them, and show that halving them does not change the conclusions.

What to record

For each study, give a table with: parameter, value, kind (derived / sourced / assumed), source or derivation, and the sensitivity run that covers it. The Australian LV study README follows this format.
