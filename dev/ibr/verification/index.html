Verification and benchmark cases

A circuit-aware optimisation model should be tested at four levels: algebraic identities, closed-form topology limits, published cases, and a higher-fidelity oracle. A solver returning LOCALLY_SOLVED is necessary, but not sufficient.

Independent physical acceptance

Every standalone advanced, constrained or controlled inverter solve, including fleet devices, now produces physical_feasibility(result). The report recomputes SI quantities from RMS voltage/current phasors, rather than trusting squared-current, sequence-power or ripple auxiliaries. It checks phase and neutral current reserves; converter sequence ratings; primitive/LCL KVL and KCL; internal/GFM voltage bounds; selected-boundary VA; source-dependent DC ripple; split midpoint charge/voltage; capacitor thermal budgets; and DC-source availability supplied by the wrapper. Reported power and ripple quantities are also checked against the independent calculation. Controlled-device reports add actual-versus-commanded current residuals in amperes; constrained-device reports add declared POC P/Q bounds. The exact fitted linear-loss norm is used in the audit, making the known one-sided smoothing deficit visible rather than hiding it in a reported auxiliary.

InverterFeasibilityTolerances declares physical floors of 10 µA, 10 µV, 0.01 VA and 1 nC, plus 1e-8 times the relevant rating. These do not depend on the network power base or silently grow with solver tolerances. The rail minimum is enclosed to 1 µV by default. All tolerances are inspectable in the individual InverterLimitCheck records.

The full-cycle rail check bounds each signed inequality as a degree-two trigonometric polynomial. On each interval, Taylor's theorem supplies a lower bound using abs(f′′) ≤ hypot(c₁,s₁) + 4 hypot(c₂,s₂); evaluated points supply upper bounds. Adaptive subdivision encloses the global minimum over the full electrical cycle, including split-link fundamental and 2ω displacement. Float64 roundoff is padded. This is a numerical enclosure of the declared averaged waveform, not an interval-arithmetic proof of hardware behavior.

The standalone solvers refine the sampled hull by adding violating angles and re-solving, for at most 12 refinements. rail_refinement=false retains the sampled research formulation; the physical acceptance gate still rejects a missed rail violation. Direct stamping remains composable: call inverter_physical_feasibility(inv, handles, solve_status(model)) after an external solve and add/refine constraints in the host problem as appropriate.

solve_status(result).publishable requires solver success and physical acceptance. MOI termination/primal statuses and the solver's feasible and optimal flags remain unchanged for diagnosis. Candidate quantities may remain present when a physically rejected solver-success point is returned; a termination string alone is insufficient to select study results. Candidate reports can also diagnose unsuccessful solves without promoting them to accepted results. No-primal outcomes have an unchecked, non-passing report.

The fixed-current single-device/fleet replays used by network sensitivities and fixed-point comparisons use the same publication gate, full-cycle rail refinement and independently checked command residuals. A coarse six-angle zero-current replay once returned publishable LOCALLY_SOLVED with roughly -35 V rail headroom; that candidate now fails physical acceptance even when refinement is explicitly disabled for diagnosis. One rejected device rejects the fleet.

Sensitivity perturbations and intermediate fixed-point iterates are virtual imposed-current capability probes. They enforce the declared plant limits but do not assert a PV availability bound. The returned fixed-point plant additionally audits request availability using an upper enclosure of independently recomputed DC power. An overdrawn final point cannot publish or report convergence. This distinction permits perturbed-voltage numerical starts without calling their virtual injections transient PV dispatch. Explicit source bounds can also be applied to an individual current replay. physical_feasibility exposes the final single-device or per-device reports; equilibrium use requires both converged and a publishable final solve.

The report checks the declared inverter fundamental model and calibrated RMS reserves. Standalone PWM-enabled solves additionally require finite carrier predictions, converged reserves and actual predicted capacitor-thermal and AC total-RMS ratings. A numerical closure tolerance cannot relax those ratings; under-allocation at a binding rating triggers another update. Nonconvergence, nonfinite harmonic networks and modulation violations prevent publication while retaining the final candidate for diagnosis. These are model audits, not EMT. Network-wide feasibility, regional compliance and transient/dynamic stability remain outside this report; network replay and controller-policy checks still provide separate evidence.

The balanced three-phase fixture uses exact ±120° angles. Rounded decimal angles introduce a small U₀ and can make a strict balanced-grid LCL controller incompatible with its passive shunt, even when a converter-target plant is feasible. A separate common-mode case retains and tests that distinction.

Constrained-device acceptance additionally reconstructs POC P/Q from terminal phasors, evaluates the declared smooth VV/VW relationship numerically, checks fixed Q/PF and P/Q bounds, and checks zero/negative sequence on the selected balanced-current arm. Standalone fixed-dispatch solves also check the requested POC watts. These checks do not rely on the constrained model's lifted power or sequence auxiliaries. Exact PWL tracking errors remain separate diagnostics: the encoded smooth curve is the declared optimization policy, not an assertion of exact firmware or regional-standard behavior.

A deliberately coarse Ipopt reproduction returns raw LOCALLY_SOLVED at 60 W / 0 var for a 2,500 W / −600 var request. All hardware checks pass, but the independently reconstructed Q and fixed-dispatch checks reject publication. Separate raw-success reproductions similarly reject violated PF and volt-watt relationships. Solver tolerances do not change the physical acceptance floors.

Omitting a conductor rating does not create a hidden current bound. Numerical current characteristics use declared ratings when present, otherwise physical terminal-voltage starts (with declared voltage-rating fallback), rather than the numerical coordinate voltage base. Three-leg KCL, LCL KCL and balanced-arm equations retain physical scaling in SI and at different per-unit bases. The shifted current norm's one-sided loss deficit is capped at 0.0001 W in total across conducting legs. Phase paths retain any smaller rating-relative width. The fourth leg instead uses its share of the absolute watt budget, capped at 1 mA: its current often vanishes while the three phase currents are nonzero. This avoids needless neutral-channel curvature without changing its exact current bounds or the independent physical acceptance floors. Composite current norms evaluate conductor sums before squaring them. Expanding the square into a quadratic polynomial can otherwise produce a small negative number through floating-point cancellation at balanced neutral current. Tests exercise this cancellation in plain and staged models at different bases. The unrated 1 mA fallback alone once understated demand by about 0.30 W for a_loss=100 W/A; the unchanged exact-norm physical gate correctly rejected that raw solver success. Regression solves now accept the corrected point in SI and at 10 kVA, 1 MVA and 100 MVA bases without widening physical tolerances.

dc_source_line_frequency_diagnostics separately reports ω/2ω source currents, their RMS/peak conventions, source resistance dissipation and a normalized antiresonance cancellation screen. Independent capacitor-node KCL and period integration check these quantities. The regulated DC-link input is the mean power boundary for p_dc, request availability and DCSourceBounds; converter and modeled capacitor ESR losses are included. Upstream source/DC–DC mean-path loss, source-impedance harmonic dissipation and DC standby are outside that boundary. The source diagnostic does not add an upstream power bound, a component thermal rating or a dynamic-stability guarantee.

Voltage-aware feeder coupling

solve_voltage_aware_inverter_fleet(net, spec) couples the local staged Q→P→I₂ allocator to the native network equations. Every local allocation uses separate sensed and physical terminal voltages. The feeder map imposes grid-arm currents and the declared POC shunt exactly once. Intermediate feeder solves are virtual numerical probes without inverter hardware constraints; they are not transient dispatch samples. Returned phasors are reconstructed through the full primitive/LCL filter and independently audited against hardware, source, volt-watt and current-command limits. Publication also requires convergence of the voltage/current map and successful local allocation stages.

Native zero-equality rows are checked in SI with physical tolerances independent of the power base. This residual audit evaluates registered model rows; it is not an independent circuit implementation or an independent audit of all native operational inequalities. Small-feeder regressions separately calculate Ohm's law and KCL from returned phasors. A failed network solve, local allocation, controller voltage-domain check or iteration budget remains unpublishable and retains its explicit diagnostic reason. Failure of a local NLP does not prove plant or policy infeasibility.

operating_modes=:energized retains reactive control and AC loss imports at zero PV. :disconnected represents an open grid-side contactor, removing the entire converter/filter/POC-shunt AC model. A complete per-device dictionary supports mixed fleets. Disconnected devices have zero AC injections and no inverter physical report or modeled DC standby power. Unselected native IBRs keep their native physics. voltage_aware_inverter_rows(result) records modes, acceptance, powers, losses, current/ripple/rail quantities and sensed voltages. Only accepted rows support equilibrium comparisons.

Three-leg reactive-priority control uses Q→P→I₂ ordering and optional voltage-admittance negative-sequence requests. Four-leg PerPhaseVoltVarWatt instead preserves the direction of its three Q requests, maximizes their common service fraction, then maximizes equal POC phase active powers. The shared DC source, neutral path and full filter remain coupled physical constraints. Independent regressions cover a closed-form binding neutral rating, mixed three/four-leg fleets, neutral KCL, full LCL losses, night operation and an extreme zero-sequence-only voltage boundary. This is solver-based quasi-static reference allocation; implicit controller stamping is not provided for this family. Current-dependent ripple blending, dynamic stability, global uniqueness and real-time guarantees remain outside this capability. Warm starts and damping are numerical choices, not a transient inverter model.

Physical certificate and full-cycle enclosure tests live in test/inverter_feasibility_tests.jl. The other tests live in test/advanced_inverter_tests.jl and test/inverter_control_tests.jl, with operating constraints in test/inverter_operating_constraints_tests.jl, test/inverter_formulation_variants_tests.jl and test/inverter_formulation_reproduction_tests.jl. The executable reproduction cases state each equivalence condition and tolerance. They intentionally include balanced cases, pure sequence excitations, strong mixed unbalance, and binding limits. A balanced feeder alone cannot validate a four-wire topology because it removes the very neutral and ripple mechanisms being tested.

Numerically demanding PWM-reserve and DC-link closure regressions use the per-unit formulation. Raw SI is retained for representative, well-scaled AdvancedInverter unit-conversion checks. The coupled phase-aware controller requires per-unit stamping and rejects raw SI because its saturated convergence is not sufficiently reliable.

The controller derivations and calling conventions are documented in the phase-aware control modeling guide. This page is the canonical claim-to-test map and publication gate. In particular, neither a balanced OpenDSS comparison nor the four-leg/three-single-phase reduction validates negative-sequence control or shared-DC-link dynamics; those are mechanism tests, not universal model equivalences.

Numerical regression settings

The DC-ripple saturation and LCL current-target regressions use tol=1e-8 with nlp_scaling_method="none"; the ripple fixture additionally uses mu_strategy="adaptive" to avoid monotone-barrier stagnation on Julia 1.10. Their equations already include per-unit and device normalization. In the grid-target fixture, Ipopt's additional gradient scaling stopped at acceptable tolerance; solving with the declared row scales reaches the requested convergence criterion and satisfies the physical current/power comparisons. These tests require publishable solutions and keep their physical tolerances; the ripple assertions are active rather than skipped. This is a measured configuration for these fixtures, not a universal solver setting. Further tightening tol can encounter the formulation's numerical accuracy floor instead of improving the physical answer.

Infeasibility triage for fleet studies

Never discard a failed or non-publishable snapshot. Retain scenario identifiers, termination status, starts, and all available residuals. Re-run it in the following diagnostic order: zero controller objective, wider smoothing via continuation, relaxed experimental service commands, and finally individually relaxed physical limits. Classify the first relaxation that restores a solution as controller-equilibrium, numerical-conditioning, or physical-capability failure. Report each class as a fraction of every penetration cohort. Only a physical-limit relaxation may support a hardware-sizing conclusion, and only after multiple starts and a higher-fidelity representative case.

Published cases worth lifting next

Heidari and Geth (2024)

Their four-conductor algebraic model provides equation-level cases for:

  1. a non-zero neutral series impedance under zero-sequence current;
  2. equality between internal and external powers plus full conductor losses;
  3. 3-leg versus 4-leg feasible regions under the same unbalanced POC voltage;
  4. independent positive-, negative-, and zero-sequence current caps; and
  5. balanced internal voltage under a GFM constraint.

The present suite covers the first, second, fourth, and fifth as invariants. The first two now exercise both diagonal and mutually coupled primitive conductor matrices. A direct digitisation of their plotted three-leg/four-leg capability boundaries would add a valuable paper-to-code regression.

Deakin, Heidari, and Deng (2025)

Table V is especially useful because the voltage and six current phasor patterns are fully prescribed. It tests the unconjugated-product convention without depending on an OPF optimum. Tables VI-VII then separate formula error from PLECS controller/filter effects. All six Table VII rows are now literal regression data. For the four non-cancelling cases, analytical and PLECS 2ω powers agree within 0.3%, and the associated reported currents within 1%; the balanced active/reactive rows retain their small numerical residuals. The package separately tests its time-domain RMS capacitor current, which divides a sinusoidal Fourier magnitude by $\sqrt2$. The table's $P_{2\omega}/V_{dc}$ current convention must not be used as an RMS bank rating without that conversion. A machine-readable PLECS waveform archive would still be stronger than copied summary values.

Deakin, Heidari, and Deng (2026)

The OPF parameter sweeps in Figs. 4-6 are useful monotonic and ordering tests:

  • increasing capacitor ripple rating cannot shrink the feasible set;
  • a monolithic 4-leg link does not route neutral current through its capacitor;
  • a split link trades neutral capability against capacitor ripple headroom; and
  • reconfigurable four-leg-plus-split hardware dominates a fixed allocation when all other ratings are equal.

The first three properties are covered, including unequal split half-banks and frequency-weighted bank ratings. The final one must wait for an explicit hybrid topology; using the current :FOUR_LEG result as its surrogate would be a model error.

Mandrioli et al. (2021) and Hammami et al. (2020)

Mandrioli et al. give balanced-load RMS closed forms for sinusoidal PWM and centered continuous PWM. These are unusually clean unit-test oracles because they isolate shared-carrier correlation from the OPF optimum. The package now evaluates both formulas at three modulation indices, then separately checks current, capacitance, and switching-frequency scaling. Hammami et al. provide the complementary three-phase four-wire split-capacitor derivation. The present split-link test exercises asymmetric half-banks and the series-equivalent bus capacitance, but digitising several of their modulation/load-angle maps remains a useful independent regression target.

Mandrioli et al. (2021), Viatkin et al. (2021), and the 2023 generalization

The split-capacitor paper provides closed-form phase and neutral switching RMS currents under SPWM. Equations (15) and (27) are now literal tests at three modulation indices, including the predicted 1/(L f_sw) scaling. Viatkin et al. add an explicit neutral inductor to the four-leg circuit; the package tests their central monotonic result that increasing the neutral-to-phase inductance ratio suppresses neutral ripple. The 2023 generalized treatment covers many common-mode injections and hundreds of simulation/experimental operating conditions. Its published numeric archive would be the best next fixture for DPWM, THIPWM, and strategy-dependent neutral-inductor validation.

Comparing exact-law and smooth-law equilibria

The exact_smooth_current_residual evaluates both control laws at the smooth model's solved operating point. It measures controller-algebra smoothing error at that point, rather than the difference between two network equilibria.

A separate calculation is available through solve_inverter_control_network_fixed_point and solve_controlled_inverter_fleet_network_fixed_point. Each iteration evaluates the exact local law, fixes the resulting converter- or grid-side current targets, and re-solves the physical plant and network. The fleet routine updates all selected targets in one network solve, retaining coupling between inverters. The default initial voltage comes from the smooth controlled solve; other initial voltages can be supplied.

The routines return voltage and current residuals, convergence and cycling information, and the status of the final plant solve. The closed-loop comparison guide describes these results, network sensitivities and comparisons across initial states. Regression tests in test/closed_loop_evidence_tests.jl cover single-inverter and fleet cases, including curved characteristics, a floating neutral and failed plant solves.

These calculations compare quasi-static equilibria under the specified model. Convergence of the iteration does not establish dynamic stability or global uniqueness. Conversely, cycling or a failed plant solve does not establish that no equilibrium exists. Hardware and dynamic-model validation remain separate requirements.

Australian study checks

The Australian LV study (studies/australian_pv/README.md) combines four kinds of check. Numbers are in the generated report (studies/australian_pv/engineering_benchmark.md); earlier campaigns are listed in studies/australian_pv/HISTORY.md.

  • Rated-capability gate. Before any feeder run, each fixture plant must deliver its nameplate at the AS/NZS 4777.2 Australia A volt-var corners with apparent power or phase current, not an internal budget, as the limit. The earlier 1 A capacitor budget failed this gate (about 1.2 kVA of 6 kVA) and made every earlier export comparison capacitor-limited.
  • PF/OPF agreement. The OPF with each site's local volt-var request and volt-watt ceiling imposed at its own voltages, and without the 253 V row, must reproduce the local fixed point. The two formulations are built separately.
  • Saved-point and network audits. Every saved point is rebuilt in SI, and a separately coded Python script reconstructs feeder currents and limits.
  • Fixed-POC EMT. A separately coded averaged/switched circuit checks that the modelled capacitor-current bound encloses the switched value at the highest-stress site per condition, mode and controller group. The bound was revised after earlier misses and is checked with the same selection rule, so this is not out-of-sample validation. It omits PLL, controller and feeder dynamics, and is not a PSCAD/PLECS or hardware comparison.

The carrier-domain and DC-boundary experiments (studies/australian_pv/engineering_domain.md, engineering_boundary.md) check the carrier approximations across P/Q, sequence and modulation under the legacy fixture limits; those limits only decide which points are labelled infeasible.

Further higher-fidelity validation

For each topology, build one parameter-identical averaged or switched model in PLECS, PSCAD, or an open EMT tool and export machine-readable sweeps. A compact validation matrix should vary:

  • positive/negative/zero-sequence voltage independently;
  • active/reactive current angle and current magnitude;
  • DC voltage, capacitance, and half-bank mismatch;
  • midpoint-balancer charge authority, bandwidth, and loss;
  • phase and neutral inductance/resistance;
  • converter-side/grid-side LCL split, capacitance, damping, and grid impedance;
  • modulation index, sampling density, and switching strategy; and
  • capacitor ESR versus frequency and temperature.

Compare converter/midpoint/POC fundamental phasors, both arm currents, filter- capacitor current and damping loss, mean P/Q, $|I_0|,|I_1|,|I_2|$, neutral RMS, $|\widetilde S|$, DC-bus 2ω amplitude and phase, midpoint fundamental RMS and mean offset, each half-bank's current spectrum, temperature, and loss. Store inputs and expected outputs in a versioned CSV/JSON fixture rather than recreating a plotted curve by eye.

Suggested acceptance bands are exact/near-machine tolerance for algebraic identities, below 0.5% for closed-form ideal cases, and study-specific bands for EMT comparisons. For switched simulations, report the Fourier window, transient discard interval, RMS/peak convention, switching frequency, controller bandwidth, and whether the DC source absorbs 2ω power.

Model-risk checks before publication

Before comparing controllers or dispatch on a feeder, check that the device fixture can deliver its declared nameplate (the rated-capability gate above). An internal budget that binds below nameplate dominates every comparison.

For every claimed boundary point:

  1. check the per-unit base against the device ratings. Ratings are stamped as squared per-unit inequalities, and Ipopt's bound_relax_factor floors its relaxation at max(1, |bound|), so the admissible physical violation grows as s_base²: a 20 kVA rating is honoured to ~0.25 VA at s_base=1e6 but exceeded by ~2.4 kVA (12 %) at s_base=1e8. Keep s_base within a couple of decades of the ratings or pass bound_relax_factor=0. The mechanism and the durable fix are documented on AdvancedInverter. No hardware-sizing claim should be published from a base that has not been checked this way;
  2. require non-negative switching_margin, increase n_samples, and demonstrate convergence;
  3. for PWM-enabled points, require non-negative pwm_reserve_margin and pwm_modulation_margin, require every AC *_reserved value to cover its predicted ripple, then refine both sampling grids and pwm_ac_harmonics;
  4. resolve from more than one physically sensible initial point;
  5. check all reported conductor, sequence, rail, and capacitor residuals;
  6. perturb the operating point inside and outside the claimed boundary;
  7. state whether each value is RMS, peak amplitude, or Fourier magnitude; and
  8. compare at least one representative point with a higher-fidelity oracle.

For an explicit LCL case, also verify that the fundamental frequency is well separated from every passive modal resonance and that the resonance remains acceptably damped after controller delay and grid impedance are included. The reported scalar filter_resonance_hz is only an initial screening number.

The maintained sources behind these tests are listed in IBR references.

PCC rating regressions in test/inverter_power_location_tests.jl compare the legacy converter circle with net PCC capability through reduced and LCL filters, including the PCC shunt. They check SI/per-unit behavior, optional current limits, exact/smooth controller replay at both current targets, and location-aware operating, fleet and hardware-sizing reports.

PCC terminal saturation regressions

inverter_sequence_limit_tests.jl separately forces known converter-sequence currents and compares contradictory and feasible ratings at 10 kVA, 1 MVA and 100 MVA numerical bases. This checks the plant's hard bounds independently of controller backoff. Controller cases cover I₁/I₂ saturation, both targets and power boundaries, common/power/balance allocation, LCL shunt offsets, equal positive/negative ratings and fleet margin reporting. Physical Fortescue sums, DC availability and exact/smooth command agreement are checked independently.

The Australian terminal-profile fleet regression covers mean, worst-phase and sequence VV+VW at both source levels and zero/half/full PV. It uses a single 100 kVA base, adaptive Ipopt profile and 300-iteration cap without retries, then checks that a zero selection objective preserves the stressed dispatch. The network audit replays all three stressed equilibria with fixed phase P/Q. The larger PCC graph retains squared-norm coefficients as expressions and normalizes its scalar root by sqrt(d + epsilon^2). The quadratic, smoothing width and service order are unchanged; the root stays scaled to headroom for vanishing service. Simple constant-service graphs retain their prior root coordinates. The smooth maximum is anchored at a positive argument to prevent cancellation in its denominator on trial points. Convergence at other network bases remains a separate conditioning question, with failed probes retained.

The Australia A review fixture also checks mean and worst-phase sensing with the same reactive-priority allocator, converter-arm current limit and PCC apparent-power circle, under separately binding current and power limits. Capacitor thermal regressions independently reconstruct weighted RMS stress from physical 2ω and declared carrier currents, cover both power boundaries, both current targets and common/power-priority sequence allocation, and compare the exact and smooth controller commands. These tests protect allocation semantics; they do not imply that the sensing policies agree under unbalance. They also do not establish equivalence to an optimisation that retains a hard Q-curve equality when that Q request itself exceeds the available capability: the forward reactive-priority allocator clips infeasible Q before allocating P. The PCC stamp lifts the curve outputs and phase-current service directions once before reusing them in capability intersections. This preserves the equations while avoiding repeated copies of the worst-phase conflict graph when exporting a fleet model to the nonlinear solver.

PCC mode is checked against independently calculated terminal-circle and arm-current intersections for watt, var and proportional priority. Both current targets must deliver the same net P/Q with filter compensation; positive and negative PCC shunts are included once. Exact replay includes the physical plant measurements and compares the quadratic segment law with its smooth interior approximation. A plant-backed network replay refreshes those measurements at each iteration. These checks validate the idealised policy, not manufacturer firmware or AS/NZS 4777.2 certification.

The review-derived fixture in test/inverter_pcc_review_tests.jl specifies a neutral-connected LCL filter on an ideal balanced four-wire source. It checks the declared Australia A curves at knots, slopes and the analytic rating/volt-watt crossover, with DC availability at 1.2, 0.5 and zero times the rating. Independent softplus formulas separate curve bias from terminal saturation and component losses. Width-halving tests check the signed corner error, and separate arm ratings check feasible-Q priority and the absence of an implied grid-arm cap. These reproducible cases do not establish general unbalanced-grid behaviour, charging control, dynamic performance or certification.
