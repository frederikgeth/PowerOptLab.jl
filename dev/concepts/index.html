Concepts

PowerOptLab studies four-wire distribution networks in which measurements, parameters or operating conditions are uncertain. The network equations use RMS voltage and current phasors, with an explicit neutral conductor. They are based on the BMOPFTools current–voltage formulation.

The intended research sequence is to estimate the network state and parameters, identify additional measurements or operating-point changes that reduce the uncertainty, and test operating decisions against the remaining possible models. The present solvers address parts of that sequence. Joint topology and parameter identification and automatic experiment design remain research objectives. The research programme describes those objectives.

The nonlinear operating-envelope solvers test specified scenarios and utilisation points. The static operability methods also provide sufficient fixed-point conditions for supported network models. These establish different results: finite scenario tests do not establish feasibility over a continuous uncertainty set, and a fixed-point condition applies only to its specified model and region.

Models, formulations and solution methods

A component model describes a device, a problem formulation specifies the network calculation, and a solution method defines the numerical procedure. These distinctions help identify what changes between two studies. The source folders follow the same three categories; the documentation groups them by study topic.

  • A component model adds the electrical equations and ratings of a device. Its terminal currents enter network KCL; storage models also introduce a state equation between time intervals.
  • A problem formulation defines an objective and constraints over the network model. Examples include OPF, state estimation and multi-period scheduling.
  • A solution method determines how those equations are solved. HELM, for example, constructs a power series from the network admittance matrix.

Study assumptions and development status

The metadata at the start of a model guide describes its development status and intended use:

  • Maturity — proof of concept (demonstrates a narrow idea), prototype (test-backed but experimental), experimental (an evolving model/interface with explicitly documented validation limits), research prototype (intended for declared studies rather than operational use), or promotion candidate (stable enough to propose for the BMOPF spec).

  • Direction — forward (given parameters, find an operating point: dispatch/OPF), inverse (given measurements, find the state or parameters), or hierarchical (different decision-makers or response levels).

  • Temporal structure — single-snapshot vs inter-temporal (state-of-charge coupling, shared parameters, or per-interval envelopes spanning many snapshots).

  • Uncertainty/security treatment — deterministic, explicit scenarios, tested utilization points, or globally certified. These labels describe different levels of assurance. State which one was used when reporting results.

Units and solution status

Inputs and results are expressed in physical units, including W, var, Wh, V and A. The OPF-based models use the BMOPFTools bases obtained with opf_bases(ctx) for per-unit scaling. Solvers that support both per_unit=true and per_unit=false retain the same physical units at their interfaces. Numerical scaling, convergence and the local solution reached can differ. Coupled phase-aware inverter-control modelling currently requires per-unit coordinates; its parameters and results remain SI-valued. Check each solver's supported formulation.

The solvers use the following common definitions:

  • AbstractDevice describes how a device is checked, added to the network equations, linked across intervals and reported in the results;
  • TimeGrid, which gives every snapshot an explicit positive duration;
  • AbstractMeasurement describes a measurement value and its uncertainty;
  • build_multi_context, which builds ordered snapshots into one JuMP model, plus solve_status and solve_diagnostics for convergence information and numerical checks.

Results from the JuMP solvers retain a SolveStatus, including the termination and primal feasibility statuses. Check solve_status(result).publishable before reading the reported solution. This flag applies the solver-convergence criteria used by the package; physical residuals and operating limits still need to be checked for the study.

See Contributing for adding models and methods, documenting their validation and proposing established work for inclusion in BMOPFTools.
